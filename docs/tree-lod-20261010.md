# Forest LOD and lighting

- Each stable tree ID receives an offset in [-20,20] metres around the quality-level distance. A 35-metre smoothstep band determines mesh coverage.
- Selection updates every 150 ms; coverage updates every frame, limited to a 1.1-second full handoff. Mesh and billboard use exactly complementary screen-space dither thresholds and the same per-tree coverage. No per-vertex versus billboard-centre distance mismatch.
- The budget ranks by staggered distance with 5 m retention hysteresis. Departing trees fade before releasing their slot. A quality downgrade can retain the previous budget briefly while those trees fade; no additional slots are admitted until under the new budget. No global quality-change forest flash.
- Reuse instanced geometry buffers across membership changes. Clone only the tree geometry that needs per-batch coverage attributes; keep ground geometry identities intact for saved placement IDs.
- Shared object-space vertex shading darkens the canopy interior and lower branches in both the mesh and baker. Rebuilt all three models' atlas at the existing 192 pixels/view, 8 azimuths and 3 elevations. This is approximate occlusion, not real-time self-shadowing.
- A 1024-square terrain-only canopy mask provides persistent soft contact shadows without shadow-map draws. It rebuilds with tree edits/density, independent of LOD. It does not cast onto buildings or animate with the sun.

Limits: discrete atlas viewpoints and baked light orientation can still reveal some silhouette/light changes. This pass softens mesh handoffs, not atlas frame interpolation. Dither avoids transparency sorting but can be visible at low resolution. Hardware performance should still be checked on the user's thin client.

## Medium/gaming presets

Auto now starts at Medium and still adapts down for slow systems. Minimal and Low disable real-time shadows; Medium/High/Ultra use 1024/2048/4096 shadow maps, clamped to device limits. The shadows menu can disable shadows or change resolution on Medium and above. Near mesh trees cast and receive shadows; buildings cast and receive, and the terrain receives them. Tree depth passes use the same coverage fade. A texel-snapped map follows the camera, updating at most 10 times/second. Distant impostors retain the inexpensive canopy ground mask. The mask and baked canopy shading remain available at every quality level.

The real-time shadow boundary is feathered before the edge of the camera-following map, avoiding a second traveling line. Switching shadows off releases its GPU render target.

Validation: all eight Node test files pass. Browser checks exercise shared mesh/impostor coverage, a 120 m camera jump, release to a 24-tree budget, and real-time shadows switching off on Low and on again on Medium. WebGL1 and WebGL2 shader/render checks are included.
